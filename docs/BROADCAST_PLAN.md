# A watchable season in about 48 real hours: plan and isolated demonstration

Status: PLAN plus an isolated demonstration (branch `broadcast/season-48h-2026-11-04`). Nothing here is deployed. Production, the existing replays, the review demo,
every backup and the league (stopped at event 188) are unchanged; no engine file and nothing under `site/` is touched. Deploying any of it, starting the timer or
resuming the league are separate decisions of the owner.

## 1. Summary

* **The year fits.** One DFL year is 47 events, 400 games in 106 kickoff windows. With a 15-minute game slot the whole broadcast (offseason opening through Honors and
  year close) takes **39.1 h**, leaving **8.9 h of reserve** inside 48 h. The offseason is 49 minutes. Calendar order and every simultaneous kickoff group are kept.
* **Simulation and broadcast are separated.** The runner computes (it already does); a new *broadcast layer* decides when finished data becomes public. Unreleased data
  is not hidden behind a rule: it is **not in the public directory**, so a direct request for it is a plain 404.
* **The current replay files cannot be the live broadcast** (each holds every play and the final score), and the current feed reveals a whole week, including
  injuries and standings, at the moment of commit. So live games are served as *protected segments* and the feed release is built privately and revealed on the clock.
* **One authoritative clock**, a pure function of its anchor, pauses and a heartbeat lease. Pause stands still. Downtime is recorded as a pause: nothing is caught up and
  nothing is skipped.
* **Demonstration built and tested** (24 unit tests, a real-browser check, a run against real archive-copy games on the Droplet): late join, refresh, synchronized
  viewers, pause/resume, `kill -9` restart, and spoiler scans of the whole public tree, including direct requests for unreleased data.
* **Smallest necessary additions** (section 7): a timetable generator, a build step after each commit, the release daemon, a honours public file, a Caddy rule for two
  new paths, and the frontend "Watch now" view (ChatGPT's; contract in `docs/BROADCAST_FRONTEND_CONTRACT.md`). No engine change is required.

Decisions needed from the owner are in section 10.

## 2. What was measured (not divided)

`tools/broadcast_measure.py`, read-only, over the 1,600 published replays (4 seasons) with the viewer's own constants (a play with movement lasts `trace.t_end + 1.2 s`,
any other play 2.6 s; speed 1). League compute times are from the league's `events.jsonl`.

| | games | min | median | p90 | p99 | max (s at 1x) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| regular season | 1440 | 542 | 667 | 730 | 796 | 849 |
| Ambassador Tour | 112 | 583 | 670 | 743 | 793 | 802 |
| playoffs | 40 | 586 | 666 | 738 | 859 | **905** |
| Ambassador Bowl | 4 | 620 | 654 | 677 | 684 | 685 |
| Coronation | 4 | 623 | 640 | 651 | 654 | 654 |
| by window: Sat 17:30 / Sun 10:00 / Sun 13:00 / Sun 17:30 / Mon 17:30 | 104/708/484/152/104 | | 676 / 668 / 664 / 666 / 662 | | 794 / 801 / 785 / 813 / 776 | 819 / 833 / 820 / 849 / 788 |

* A game is **about 11 minutes** at 1x (median 667 s); the longest of 1,600 is 15.1 minutes. About 68 % of plays have recorded movement; the rest are cards (kickoff,
  punt, penalty, timeout, score ...) at 2.6 s each, which is why plays-times-a-constant would have been wrong.
* **Game length does not depend on the window** (medians within 14 s), so one slot length serves every window.
* League compute: a week event about 70 s, a tour round / wild card / divisional about 16-18 s, everything else 1-9 s. The league is therefore always far ahead of any
  broadcast (a week of broadcast is 110 minutes) and **needs no speed-up to keep up**.

## 3. The 48-hour timetable

Generated by `tools/broadcast_timetable.py` from `data/OFFSEASON.csv` and the Season 1 calendar, schedule and tour tables (weekday and kickoff are the same every season).
Times are broadcast hours from the offseason opening; a wall-clock rendering takes `--wall <start>`.

| Block | Broadcast time | Length |
|---|---|---|
| Offseason, 11 events (opening ... waivers) | 0:00 - 0:49 | 49 min |
| Weeks 1 - 4 (each 5 windows + results) | 0:49 - 8:09 | 110 min per week |
| Break (calendar week 5) | 8:09 - 8:17 | 8 min |
| Weeks 5 - 8, trading deadline | 8:17 - 15:42 | |
| Week 9 | 15:42 - 17:32 | 110 min |
| Break week with Tour round 4 (4 windows) + results | 17:32 - 19:01 | 89 min |
| Weeks 10 - 14 | 19:01 - 28:11 | |
| Break (calendar week 17) | 28:11 - 28:19 | 8 min |
| Weeks 15 - 19 (bye-group weeks) | 28:19 - 37:29 | 110 min per week |
| Wild card (2 windows of 2 games) | 37:29 - 38:16 | 47 min |
| Divisional (2 windows of 2 games) | 38:16 - 39:03 | 47 min |
| Conference championships (1 window, 2 games) | 39:03 - 39:29 | 26 min |
| Ambassador Bowl + ceremony and rings | 39:29 - 39:55 | 26 min |
| Coronation + ceremony | 39:55 - 40:21 | 26 min |
| Honors and year close | 40:21 - 40:51 | 30 min |

(The table is the output at a 16-minute slot; at the proposed 15-minute slot every window is a minute shorter: total **39.08 h**.)

**Rules.** A *window* is one kickoff group: its games start together and the viewer switches between them. Regular games and Tour games of the same calendar week
share windows (week 1: 1+1 Sat, 10+1 Sun 10:00, 6+1 Sun 13:00, 2 Sun 17:30, 1+1 Mon). Every window has the same fixed length whatever is in it, so the schedule itself
reveals nothing about a game's length. Between windows there is a 5-minute gap (scores, next-kickoff countdown); after a week's last window a 10-minute results block,
when that week's feed release is revealed (section 5). The year is 400 games in 106 windows.

**Slot length.** The slot must hold the longest game minus a 30 s tail. At 900 s every measured game plays at 1.00x except the longest few (905 s: 1.04x, imperceptible).
A game that would not fit is sped up by exactly the factor it needs, never more; a game that fits is never sped up.

| slot | total | reserve in 48 h | factor for median / p99 / longest |
|---|---|---|---|
| 13 min | 35.6 h | 12.4 h | 1.00 / 1.06 / 1.21 |
| 14 min | 37.3 h | 10.7 h | 1.00 / 1.00 / 1.12 |
| **15 min (proposed)** | **39.1 h** | **8.9 h** | 1.00 / 1.00 / 1.04 |
| 16 min | 40.9 h | 7.2 h | 1.00 / 1.00 / 1.00 |
| 17 min | 42.6 h | 5.4 h | 1.00 / 1.00 / 1.00 |

**Challenge to the target.** 48 h is a ceiling, not a goal worth filling: nobody watches 39 hours, and the season is a river with drop-in viewing (the busiest window,
Sunday 10:00, has ten games at once and lasts 15 minutes; the quiet Saturday and Monday windows have one or two). The reserve is what makes a *no-catch-up* policy affordable
(section 6). Using it up on longer slots would make every pause push the season past 48 h.

## 4. How it works

```
 league runner (existing)  -->  private store (never served)  -->  broadcast clock decides  -->  public tree (static files)
 computes events, commits       feed releases, full replays,       what may exist now             clock.json, schedule.json,
                                segments, timetable with results                                  live/<game>/..., replays/..., feed/...
```

**Private store and public tree.** Everything computed lives in a private directory outside the web root. The public tree is *only* what the clock has released. The daemon
places files at their release time and removes files that are not yet due (so a restart or a rewound clock is repaired the same way). A viewer's request for anything else is
an ordinary 404, whatever the URL. Caddy's existing `@hidden` rule and file-based matching apply unchanged; the only new Caddy lines are `no-store` for `clock.json` and each
live `manifest.json`.

**Clock.** `position = anchor_pos + (min(now, lease_until) - anchor_wall)` while running; `anchor_pos` while paused. `clock.json` is public; viewers take server time from
the `Date` header. The daemon renews `lease_until` (90 s ahead) every few seconds. Viewers re-read the clock at least every 15 s. If the daemon dies, every viewer's clock stops
at the lease end (no viewer runs ahead of released data); when the daemon returns it records the gap as a pause and carries on from the position where the clocks stopped.
A pause is `pause`/`resume` records in an append-only log (`clock_log.jsonl`), so the position at any moment can be reconstructed and audited.

**Live segments.** A game kicks off at its window start. Its plays are laid on a timeline using the viewer's own durations (divided by the speed factor if needed) and cut
into 5-second segments by the time each play *starts*; segment *n* is released when the game is *n x 5 s* old. A viewer shows game time `position - kickoff - 3 s`. Files:
`head.json` (teams, roster at kickoff, field; no final, winner, overtime flag, play count or length), `manifest.json` (released segment count, state), `s0000.json` ...,
and, only after the last play has been shown, `end.json` (final, winner, play count, name of the full replay) plus the full replay in the on-demand library.
Plays keep the existing replay schema (checked against the exporter's allow-list) plus `t0`/`dur`, so the existing viewer can draw them.

**Late join and refresh.** The shown state is a function of the public files and the position only (`viewer_state`). A late joiner or a refresh reads `clock.json`, the manifest
and the segments already released and lands exactly where everybody else is; there is no per-viewer server state.

**Completed broadcasts become replays.** At the end the full replay is placed in `broadcast/replays/` and listed in a replay library index that lists only ended games. Under
this plan the *broadcast layer* owns publishing replays (today the exporter does, gated by the revealed feed), so a game becomes watchable on demand when its broadcast ends and
not when its week's feed is revealed.

**Runner lead.** The runner is not changed. To bound how far the league is ahead, the daemon creates and removes the runner's existing `PAUSED` file: the league may be at most
2 events ahead of the event being broadcast (a week and its Tour round are two events). A failure then costs at most unbroadcast events, not a season of them.

## 5. Spoiler model

Protected until released: plays, scores, results, winners, overtime, game length and play count, box scores, standings, injuries, rosters after the game, tours' tables,
awards, honours, Bowl rings. Mechanisms:

| Surface | Protection |
|---|---|
| live game data | per-segment release by file placement; `end.json` and the full replay only after the last play was shown |
| feed (`latest.json`, releases, `status.json`) | built in a private feed root after each commit; the **week's release is revealed whole at that week's results block, its pointer last**; `status.json` reports only revealed events |
| existing replay files and index | unchanged on disk and public as of today; new ones enter the library only when their broadcast ends |
| downloadable files | everything downloadable is inside the public tree, so the same rule holds |
| rosters / later roster changes | the head's roster is the roster at kickoff, built before any later event; later changes are in a later feed release |
| awards, honours | **not exported today**; a new public `honors.json` is built privately at year close and revealed at the Honors block (decision 4) |
| the schedule | public by design: windows, kickoffs, matchups; no lengths, no results |

**Tests** (`tests/test_broadcast.py`, 24): at about 125 positions across both windows a scan of every byte of the public tree finds no play released later than its segment, no final,
winner, overtime flag, play count or length, and no full replay, and an unreleased play is found in no public file; direct requests for unreleased segments, `end.json`,
the full replay under its real name, the private store, `..` and `%2e%2e` traversal, hidden files, and non-GET methods all fail; the feed release does not exist before its
reveal and its pointer is replaced last. Mutation check: releasing one segment early or the ending 8 s early makes the spoiler tests fail.

**Residual exposure (honest).** (1) Anyone watching the network tab sees at most about **8 s (5 s segment + 3 s delay, roughly one play)** ahead of the screen. (2) League
tables, team pages and injury lists update **once per week** (at the results block), not after each game; per-game partial reveals would need a feed-format change. A finished
game's final score is on its replay-library entry and in `end.json` as soon as it ends. (3) Anyone who records a live segment can of course share it. (4) The existing public
`status.json` reports `league_events` and the runner's `seq` (events committed); with the league ahead of the broadcast that tells viewers how far it has computed (never a result). It must report revealed events only.

## 6. Pause, restart, downtime and the 48-hour tradeoff

* **Pause** (operator): position stands still; no files are released or removed; viewers show "paused" (`clock.state`). **Resume** continues from the same position.
* **Restart** of the daemon (crash, deploy, reboot): files and clock are on disk. Inside the 90 s lease, nothing is noticed. Beyond it, viewers' clocks stopped at the lease
  end and the gap is logged as a pause. **No catch-up burst, no skipped viewing**: the timetable is never compressed into the gap, and a game that was on air when the clock
  stopped resumes at the same play.
* **Runner stopped, daemon up**: the broadcast continues from what is built; the lead cap means up to two events of buffer.
* **The tradeoff.** Preserving every broadcast means every minute of stoppage moves the rest of the season later. The end time is *planned end + total stoppage*.
  With 8.9 h of reserve the season still ends inside 48 h if the total stoppage over the year is under 8.9 h. Beyond that there are two choices, decided by the owner when it
  happens: finish after 48 h, or shorten *only non-game time* (the 5-minute gaps and 10-minute results blocks total 10.6 h at the proposed lengths; compressing them to
  half would recover about 5 h more, bringing tolerated stoppage to about 14 h) by an operator-recorded "gap scale" in the clock log. Game slots are never shortened.
  Not recommended: skipping windows or running games faster to catch up, which is exactly the skipped viewing the requirement forbids.

## 9. Decisions left in the plan (owner)

1. **Slot length**: 15 min (proposed, 39.1 h) or 16 min (every game at exactly 1x, 40.9 h).
2. **Playoff windows**: the data has no kickoff times for them. Proposed: wild card and divisional as 2 windows of 2, conference championships as 1 window of 2, Bowl and Coronation
   alone with a ceremony each.
3. **Results granularity**: standings, injuries and rosters update once per week (proposed), or after each window (needs a feed-format change).
4. **Honors**: which items appear at the Honors block and in what order; they need a new public file.
5. **Downtime tolerance** beyond the reserve (section 6): finish late, or compress non-game time.
6. **Public path** for the broadcast files (proposed `/feed/broadcast/`) and a review URL for the demo, which needs a Caddy change that was not authorized.
