# Game engine (Phase 3)

Authority: Design Document Ed 8.46, section 10 (rules) and 17 (determinism). Code in `src/dfl/engine/`, parameters in `data/engine/`, tests in `tests/test_engine.py`, calibration in `tools/calibrate_engine.py`.

## What it is
A **play-level** engine. The rules core is exact to section 10 (four 15-minute quarters, 40-second play clock, three timeouts a half, no coin toss, kickoffs from the 35 with touchbacks to the 25, free kick after a safety, scoring, the 46-foul penalty catalogue (Edition 8.46), the two-minute warning, overtime of one 10-minute period with a possession for each side and a defensive score ending it, successive 15-minute periods in the postseason). How a play *turns out* is a statistical model driven by the hidden attributes of the 22 players on the field. It is **not** the spatial engine of section 10 (positions, routes, ball flight); that replaces the play resolver later behind the same interface, and the calibration bands below are what it must keep meeting.

Players come from character cards (`dfl.characters`). A `Squad` picks the best available player at each position (4-3 defense; QB, HB, TE, 3 WR, 5 OL), turns their attributes into unit ratings (quarterback, pass protection, receivers, back, run blocking, pass rush, coverage, run defense, kicker, punter), and the play model reads the matchups as z-scores against a league baseline stored in `PARAMS.csv`. Coaches (OC, DC, head coach) add a small capped effect. Nothing hidden leaves the engine: the result carries stats and a play log only.

## Builder defaults where the spec is silent (change on request)
- Pass/run mix from down, distance, score, clock; hurry-up when trailing late; clock-milking and kneel-downs when protecting a lead.
- Fourth down: punt, field goal (about 55 yards, kicker-dependent) or go; going more often when trailing late.
- Extra point by default; a two-point try when down 2 after the touchdown, and sometimes late when down 5, 8 or up 1. No tries in overtime.
- A first down briefly stops the clock (section 10); the model gives the next snap 26-34 s instead of 30-40 s.
- Penalties (Edition 8.46): 46 fouls from `data/engine/PENALTIES.csv`, rates fitted to the NFL's 2023 accepted counts by type (`tools/calibrate_penalties.py`; 5.94 a team a game, 50 yards). A live-ball foul is accepted or declined by whichever side it does not hurt; dead-ball fouls repeat the down; fouls after the play (face mask, unnecessary roughness, roughing the passer) are added to where the play ended; intentional grounding costs the down; the half-the-distance rule near the goal; each foul is charged to a player (position-weighted draw) and a second unsportsmanlike foul ejects her. Kicking-play fouls move the receiving team's spot, replay the kick or give a first down. What is left out is in `docs/ENGINE_COVERAGE.md`.
- Between-play delays moved one second longer in Edition 8.46 (31 to 41 seconds, 27 to 35 after a first down) because the extra dead-ball fouls squeezed plays per team to the top of its band (68.0 against 60 to 68); they are now 66.8.
- Two-minute warning: the clock stops at 2:00 in the second and fourth quarters, no timeout charged (the NFL's rule; section 10 is silent).
- Overtime possession guarantee applies in each postseason period (the spec's wording is "the same possession guarantee").
- Timeouts: taken late in a half by a side that is behind (or the tied side at the end of the first half).
- Injuries: a small per-snap rate weighted by durability; injured players are out for the rest of the game; weeks are drawn from a table. A short position is filled by the best leftover player (the out-of-position penalty is still open).

## Not modeled yet (play-level; the spatial engine is described at the end)
Challenges and replay, fatigue and substitution patterns, nickel and dime packages, playbooks and game plans, special-teams returns beyond a distribution, the two-minute warning, formations as such, weather, individual defensive stats, All-Star composite squads (the Ambassador Bowl needs them; the people layer decides who is on them).

## Calibration (Ed 8.16 parameters, 10,000 games per seed, 48 founding-league clubs)
Targets are NFL aggregate bands in `data/engine/BANDS.csv`. Of 24 bands, seed 1 misses 2, seed 2 misses 2, seed 3 misses 3; the misses are marginal (punts per team 4.8-5.2 against 5.0, tie rate 1.4-1.6% against 1.5%, margin spread 15.8 against 15.5, and on seed 3 completion rate, yards per attempt and yards per carry about 0.1 over). Different seeds make different clubs, so some spread across seeds is expected. These are **placeholders tuned to the NFL's aggregates**, not evidence about the DFL: parity, home edge and tie frequency need the people layer and Phase 4.

**Tie frequency (spec open item 7).** With a 10-minute overtime and the both-sides possession rule the engine ties about 1.5% of regular-season games, and about 21% of overtime games end level. That is higher than the NFL's recent rate, because both sides must get the ball and a game can end level after a field goal each. **The owner accepted this rate (2026-10-23); the overtime rule stands.** Re-measure with the spatial engine.

Speed: about 290 games per second, so a 1,056-game season takes about four seconds.

## Wired into the season loop (2026-10-23)
`EngineResolver` (`src/dfl/engine/resolver.py`) plugs the engine into `league.play_season`: one squad per club from character cards, injuries carried from game to game, All-Star composite squads for the Ambassador Bowl (best four per position across the conference's Tour clubs, until the people layer's voting exists). `python3 tools/run_seasons.py --engine` runs it.

100 seasons, seed 1, **static rosters** (no draft, aging, free agency or retirement between seasons): 0 validator failures, win-percentage spread 0.18 (the NFL's is about 0.19), year-to-year win% correlation 0.40, about 5.7 ties a season. But the league is not fair over time: individual clubs were exiled between 1 and 48 times in 100 years, 12 clubs won every title and one club won 24. **That is the expected result of frozen rosters, not evidence about the DFL's parity machinery.** It is the baseline the draft, the lottery, the cap and the schedule must beat, and it is why the next step is connecting cards to the ledger (draft classes, development, aging, retirement) so the fairness bands can be measured on a living league.

## Playing from the living ledger (Ed 8.18)
`LedgerEngine` (`src/dfl/engine/ledger_engine.py`) replaces the static squads: each game's squad is the ledger's game-day active list as cards (elevations and emergency players included) with the club's coaches; injuries go back to the ledger (`World.injure`) and cost real games; postseason rounds heal a week each. Over 40 living years (draft, development, aging, free agency) the league holds steady: about 20.5 points per team game, win% spread 0.21, 5.7 ties a season, 24 different champions. Details in `docs/BUILD_STATUS.md`. Scoring is a little under the NFL's 22 to 23 because the calibration was done on the founding league; recalibrate on the living league when the spatial engine arrives.

## Box scores and the play table (Ed 8.47)
`dfl.engine.boxscore` turns a finished game into a box score (`box_score`, `format_box`), a scoring summary and play-by-play text (`play_by_play`). Try it with `python3 tools/show_game.py --seed 4 --pbp`. Play with `log=True` to get the structured play table (`GameResult.table`, one dict per event, players by id; the older tuple log `GameResult.plays` is unchanged). Individual defensive credit (tackles, assists, sacks, tackles for loss, passes defended, interceptions, forced and recovered fumbles) is drawn on its own random stream, by position group and the defender's skill, so keeping or dropping the table or the credit never changes a score (tested); the spatial engine will name the player who actually made the play. Kicking (field goals with long, extra points, kickoffs, touchbacks), punting (average, inside the 20, touchbacks) and returns (kickoff and punt) are credited to the kicker, punter and the squad's returner. Conventions: a touchdown run or catch counts the yards to the goal line only, a kneel-down is a one-yard loss rush, the half-the-distance rule is applied to every foul that would carry the ball more than half the way to the goal.

## The 22-player (spatial) engine (Ed 8.48 and 8.49)
Opt-in: `play_game(..., spatial=True)` plays every run and pass snap with 22 players moving on a 100-yard field plus end zones, 20 times a second; `trace=True` also keeps each snap's compressed trace in the play table for the 2D view. Since Ed 8.52 it is the league's default (`Universe(spatial=True)`); the play-level engine stays for speed and its golden digest is unchanged. Penalties, injuries, the clock, kicking and the box score are shared code.

Code (`src/dfl/engine/spatial/`): `core.py` (field, data files, player physical profiles), `align.py` (formations and defensive alignments), `calls.py` (play and coverage calls from the situation only), `sim.py` (the step loop, movement, blocking engagements, contact), `runplay.py`, `passplay.py`, `defense.py`, `carry.py` (ball carrier, tackles, fumbles, the outcome), `trace.py` (decode a trace; `to_json` for a viewer). Every number is data in `data/engine/spatial/` (`PHYSICS.csv`, `PLAYS.json`, `ROUTES.json`, `FORMATIONS.json`, `COVERAGES.json`). Speed: about 1.2 games a second on one core (a play-level game is about 290 a second).

Tools: `tools/calibrate_spatial.py` (snap level: draws game situations from play-level games, uses the real play calling, reports run and pass targets, `--detail` for the breakdown), `tools/fit_spatial.py` (coordinate-descent fit of the physics over four founding leagues), `tools/calibrate_engine.py --spatial --procs 2` (the 24 game bands), `tools/show_game.py --spatial --traces DIR` (one game with every snap's 2D trace written as JSON).

**Measured (Ed 8.52):** 24 of 24 game bands pass at the default check (seed 1, 1,000 games) and at seeds 14 and 15 (1,500 games each). At seeds 16 and 17, founding leagues with stronger offenses, completion rate (0.674, 0.675 against 0.67) and yards per attempt (7.63, 7.65 against 7.6) run over, and at 17 yards per carry (4.95 against 4.8). The play-level engine also misses two or three bands on some seeds, so a spread across founding leagues is expected, but it is wider here (spec section 20 item 50). The seven bands missed in Ed 8.49 closed through: a gap-fill and leverage model for run defenders, accuracy and deep-throw catch penalties, a receiver's post-catch speed, defender turn timing, a quarterback who throws past the first-down marker on third down, first-down play calling that no longer boosts deep concepts, a pass-concept mix weighted toward the plays that move the chains, a spatial injury rate, and a realistic fumble rate; then three refits of `tools/fit_spatial.py`. The pass-concept weights matter most: play-action succeeded 35 percent of the time and was sacked 12 percent, quick slants 64 percent and 0.5 percent.

The engine is sensitive to the league's average ratings (a point or two on receivers, quarterbacks or pass rushers moves passing yards per attempt by 0.3 to 0.7), which is why the fit uses four founding leagues.

Not built (item 47): other personnel groupings, nickel and dime, fatigue and substitution, on-field kicking and returns, challenges and replay, contact injuries, penalties tied to the players' positions.

## Neutral venues (owner ruling 2026-10-29)

A game dict may carry `neutral: true`. The engine then gives the nominal home side no home-field edge (owner ruling: neutral venues provide no home-field advantage). Opening possession is unaffected: spec section 10 says "Home kicks to start the first half, away the second. No coin toss. In overtime the away side kicks first", and at a neutral site "home" is the nominal home that sections 6 and 7 assign. Used for all 28 Tour games, the Bowl (nominal home by a seeded draw, owner ruling), and a Coronation held at a venue that is not a finalist's own. `tools/calibrate_engine.py --informational` is the smoke-run mode used by CI: band misses are printed, crashes still fail.
