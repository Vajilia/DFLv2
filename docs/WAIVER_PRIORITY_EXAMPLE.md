# Worked example: waiver claim order of all 48 clubs (seed 1, Season 2 = league year 2001)

Generated by `python3 tools/show_waiver_example.py --seed 1` (about 30 seconds; the same seed gives the same table). Season 2 is the first season with a real previous draft order, so it is the first in which the transition can be seen.

## What "prior draft slot" means (owner ruling L1)

It is **the club's own slot**, not a traded pick. The previous season's draft order is a list of *clubs*: the eight returning Ambassadors in lottery order, then the non-playoff clubs worst record first, then the playoff clubs by how far they went. A draft pick is a (year, round, original club) token and the slot it takes depends on its original club alone; owning another club's first-round pick changes who *selects* at that slot, never whose waiver place it is. `Universe.waiver_priority` reads only the list of clubs and never looks at pick ownership, so **trading picks cannot transfer waiver priority** (proved at the end of this page and by `tests/test_waiver_priority.py`).

Two periods, one rule each:

1. **Until four league weeks have finished** the order is last season's draft order, club by club.
2. **From the paid week after league week 4** the 40 clubs at home are ordered by this season's standings, worst record first (the league tiebreak cascade, the seeded draw last). The eight Ambassador clubs have no place in the home standings, so each keeps exactly the place her own draft slot gave her; the home clubs fill the remaining 40 places in standings order.

## Pick trades do not move waiver priority

Immediately afterwards, in the same league, `San Diego` (place 1) and `Seattle` (place 48) swapped their 2002 first-round picks. `San Diego` now owns `Seattle`'s pick and `Seattle` owns `San Diego`'s. The waiver order recomputed after the trade is **identical** in all 48 places: True.
